Tages the Etruscan Bible
Abstract
There is no preserved the Etruscan Bible written in authentic Etruscan language. However, the Roman sources testify about an ancient prior-to-the-bible biblical tradition cultivated among the Etruscans from remote antiquity. Consequently, the Etruscan biblical tradition is of extraordinary significance not only for the Etruscans themselves, but equally for the whole Aryan Indo-European speaking people. The Etruscan tradition being of mythical nature was understandably misunderstood by the Roman writers of the time. In spite of their complete unawareness of its significance, by recording the tradition for the posterity they did a great favor to the preservation of Aryan antiquities, otherwise destined to oblivion.

The Etruscan Bible vs Septuaginta
The conquest of all the known world in 4th century BCE by Alexander of Macedon
introduced Greek as lingua franca of ancient world and a new cultural syncretism merging Greek tradition with other cultures from Mediterranean to India, labeled Hellenistic period. The close contacts thus established aroused interest in cultures and traditions other than Greek, which led to the foundation of the Great Library of Alexandria in Egypt, as a depository of all the knowledge of the ancient world. One
of the main projects related to was the demand for the translation of the Pentateuch (viz. Five Books of Moses) or the Torah “Law” from Biblical Hebrew into Greek by Ptolemy II Philadelphus, the Greek Pharaoh of Egypt, in the 3rd century BCE, for its inclusion into the common treasure of knowledge at the Library of Alexandria.
The earliest translation of the Hebrew Bible into foreign languages known in Latin
as the Septuaginta or in the original Greek Ἡ Μετάφρασις τῶν Ἑβδομήκοντα, viz. “The Translation of the Seventy”, dated from 3rd and 2nd century BCE, actually when the Etruscan culture was at its decline and total submergence into Roman civilization.
This confirms that the Etruscan biblical tradition was authentic one and much older than the Hebrew, streaching far back to the Hittite-Hurrian period, preceding Moses for several centuries at least. As is attested by the scriptures, Moses then three months
old was found in a basket left among the reeds along the bank of the Nile and was adopted by the princess Hatšepsut, who became the Pharaoh herself around 1478 BCE (cf. Ivankovic, M. 2017. Veda vs. Tetragrammaton: Decrypting the greatest Enigma in History. International Journal of Sanskrit Research, 3(1); 21-25). Since
Moses lived 120 years (cf. Deuteronomy 34:7), viz. 40 years as a prince at the Egyptian court, 40 years in exile after killing a Pharaoh’s serviceman, and 40 years wandering with the Israelites throughout the Sinai desert, he could have written the Torah not earlier than 1400 BCE.
Thus, the Etruscan biblical tradition about the First Man, otherwise known from the Chapter 2 of the First Book of Moses, called the Genesis, undoubtedly was much
older than Moses. This conclusion is further corroborated by the parallels attested in both Hurrian and Hittite traditions.



The Aryan Vedic Concept of Man
The Aryan Vedic tradition speaks repeatedly about Man as the offspring of Mother
Earth and Father Heaven, e.g.:
Rig-Veda VI 51.5: dyaùṣ pítaḥ p ̥ŕthivi mā́ tar ádhrug ... m ̥rḷátā naḥ
“O Heaven our Father, Earth our undeceitful Mother, ...have mercy on us”

dyaúḥ
“May the Wind blow to us that refreshing remedy, may Earth our Mother (give it),

Rig-Veda I 164.33: dyaúr me pitā́ janitā́ ... bándhur me mātā́ p ̥rthivī́ mahī́yám
“Heaven is my Father, my begetter ... this great earth is my kin and Mother”
The Earth especially, has always been considered Mother of Man, undoubtedly since

derived from humus “earth”, and many more examples, revealing the innate
connection between man and earth in view of the ancients.
The Etruscan Tages and the Science of Liver-Inspection
Significantly, the same concept of man as the offspring of Mother Earth also existed
among the ancient Etruscans as recorded by the Roman writers Cicero and Ovid.
Thus Marcus Tullius Cicero in his work De Divinatione (“About Divination”), Liber
Secundus, Chapters XXIII and XXIV (50-54) recorded an authentic tradition about
mysterious Tages as handed down by the Etruscans themselves, in his words:
“Haec accepimus ab ipsis, haec scripta conservant, hunc fontem habent disciplinae” viz.



“This is the story as we get it from the Etruscans themselves and as their records preserve it,
and this, in their own opinion, is the origin of their science of haruspicy.”
“Sed quid plura? Ortum videamus haruspicinae; sic facillume quid habeat auctoritatis iudicabimus. Tages quidam dicitur in agro Tarquiniensi, cum terra araretur et sulcus altius esset impressus, exstitisse repente et eum adfatus esse qui arabat. Is autem Tages, ut in libris est Etruscorum, puerili specie dicitur visus, sed senili fuisse prudentia. Eius adspectu cum obstipuisset bubulcus clamoremque maiorem cum admiratione edidisset, concursum esse factum, totamque brevi tempore in eum locum Etruriam convenisse. Tum illum plura locutum multis audientibus, qui omnia verba eius exceperint litterisque mandarint. Omnem autem orationem fuisse eam qua haruspicinae disciplina contineretur; eam postea crevisse
rebus novis cognoscendis et ad eadem illa principia referendis” viz.
“What to say more (about haruspicy)? Let us examine its origin and thus we will be ready to
judge its value. It is said that a certain Tages in the field of Tarquinii, while the earth was
being plowed, and a deeper furrow pressed, he suddenly sprung forth (from the earth) and
spoke to the plowman. Now that Tages, according to the Etruscan scriptures, is said to have
had the appearance of a boy, but the wisdom of an old man. Seeing that, the plowman
shrieked with fear whereupon a crowd gathered around him; and in a short time, the whole
of Etruria assembled at the place. Then Tages spoke more to his numerous audience, who
zealously received all that he had to say, and entrusted it to writing. His whole address was
devoted to an exposition of the science of haruspicy (viz. liver-inspection). Later, as new
things were learned with reference to the same principles, they were added to the previous
fund of knowledge.”
Cicero apparently being misled by the Etruscan over-emphasis on haruspicy, scoffed
at their haruspicinae disciplina (viz. the science of liver-inspection) in the manner
as every academic skeptic as he was would inevitably do:

The Etruscan story about a Tyrrhenus (viz. Etruscan) plowman eyewitnessing the
metamorphose (viz. transmutation) of a lump of earth into a man called Tages who
then spoke oracles and taught the Etruscans how to discover the future events, was
also delineated poetically by the popular Roman poet Publius Ovidius Naso (born 43
BCE in the same year when Cicero died) in his famous poem quite fittingly titled the
Metamorphoses (“Transmutations”), Liber XV, verses 553-559:

called him Tages, the one who first taught the Etruscans how to discover the future events.”



However, it was neither Cicero nor Ovid to blame but the Etruscan haruspices (viz.

The Etruscans were utterly practical and cared only for the Time Being, while

Tages the Man of the Earth
Both accounts on Tages, the one by Cicero, and the other by Ovid, suffer from
inadequacies, as to his true origin. Fortunately, another Roman writer, viz. Sextus
Pompeius Festus, a noted grammarian from 2nd century CE, has preserved such
invaluable data for posterity in his De Verborum Significatione (“On the Meanings
of Words”), otherwise known as the Lexicon of Festus:
“Tages nomine Genii filius, nepos Iovis, puer dicitur discipulinam aruspicii dedisse
duodecim populis Etruriae” viz.
“Tages by name, the son of Ge(nii), the offspring of Iove (Iuppiter), is said to have handed
down the science of Liver-inspection to the Twelve peoples of Etruria”
Even such a succinct formulation contains some data of paramount importance where
all other accounts failed. Thus, from the above it is perfectly clear that the authentic
Etruscan tradition held Tages to be the offspring of Heaven (viz. Iove) too, besides
his being the son of the Earth.
As for the term nepos, Etruscan neft, cognate with Lemnian naφoθ, it is mostly
misinterpreted as “grandson”, since originally it meant “descendant, offspring, whence
son” and as such attested in Vedic Sanskrit nápāt, vs Classical náptar (the former
stem only in strong cases and earlier language, the latter in Classical Sanskrit), but
also feminine naptí “daughter”; only in later language the term náptar (stem napt ̥r)
being restricted to the sense “grandson” and naptrī to “granddaughter”.
Thus, unquestionably, the phrase “nepos Iovis” denoted Tages as the offspring viz. son
of Heaven, Iove being his father. Naturally, the Earth was his Mother, for he sprung
out from the sulcus (Latin) or αὖλαξ (Greek) viz. “a furrow made by the plow”.



As for the phrase Genii filius, it is misinterpreted too, since the Genius (derived from
Ge “earth”) was believed to be already present in every individual being, place or
thing. Tages was authentically considered an offspring of Γῆ [=Gē > genius] as the
Earth was poetically called by the Greeks since Homer’s time. Even the very name
Tages testify of his being the offspring of the Earth (see further below). What is more,
the Etruscan terms denoting Earth, Man and kinship are all cognates with Indo
European ones. Thus, beside the common terms for “giving birth” the Etruscan shared
also the Indo-European terms for Man and Earth too.

As for Tyrrhenos from Lydia, the Ionian variant of his name (used by Herodotus,

Thus, the Etruscan tradition itself held that they came as newcomers to the Apennine
peninsula after migration caused by severe famine from Anatolia. But this was the
last venue of their collective memory, for they lost almost all rememberance prior to
that period with the exception of some names of ancient heroic characters, like the
Trojan hero Aeneas, and the mythical name Tages inherited from times immemorial.



However, under the conditions of sheer survival, the collective memory of the
Etruscan people became lost into the corridors of time. Fortunately, all the memory
of the bygone eras is preserved in the words of human language. Hence, the authentic
primaeval concept from which evolved the myth about Tages can be recollected again
by comparative method, for the missing links are preserved in neighboring cultures
and encoded into their languages.
As is demonstrated initially, the fundamental concept of man as the offspring of
Father Heaven and Mother Earth is preserved already by the Rig-Veda. But even
closer phonetical and semantical cognates with Etruscan Tages are found in
Anatolian languages, viz. Hurrian taγe “(hu)man” apparently related to Hittite te-e-kán
“earth”, in the oblique cases (locative singular) ta-ga-a-an, with Voiced ‘G’ internally
within the word (cf. Mayrhofer EWA I, 1992:425), of which Etruscan form Tages
represents genitive/ablative viz. “sprung from the Earth” (as specified in the story
delineated by all classical writers).
Moreover, there is a Greek variant akin to Hurrian taγe and Etruscan Tages, but
realized with all Stops VOICED, spelled δαγύς = DaGus (in the oblique cases δαγῦδος,
analogous to Τάγητος < Τάγης), originally denoting a “mannequin”, dummy, doll,
a miniature model of the human body, hence in ancient Greek the term designated
“wax doll, used in magic rites, puppet” (exactly as in voodoo magic).
As for the Stop phonemes in Etruscan, though they were phonemically Voiceless,
there have been no restriction to their realization redundantly as Voiced allophones
B,D,G, internally within the word, as the examples from Hittite testify (and also
Mandarin Chinese, and many other languages).
NB. Cf. remark by Helmut Rix, 2004:947, “The obstruents of Etruscan are phonemically voiceless. In word-initial position they were realised as fortes ([+
tense]) and internally as lenes ([− tense, −/+ son]). Latin transcriptions with p, t, c, f at the beginning of a word and b, d, g internally lead to this reconstruction (Pabassa, Tidi, Pergomsna, Fraunal, Noborsinia for Papaσa, Titi, Percumsna, Fraucnal, Nufrznei [personal names]”. Naturally, here belongs Tages too.



Etruscan Agriculturist Myth
The story about Tages has preserved several different layers of ancient myths, originally unrelated to each one. Since from the beginning the focus of the story is on the “Etruscan plowman” (viz. Tyrrhenus arator) with special emphasis on his pressing
a “deeper furrow” (viz. sulcus altius) this part of the story actually makes reference to
the discovery of technologically advanced and more efficacious method or cultivating the soil, viz. the promotion of agriculturist new technology for deeper plowing in order to secure better results in agriculture. Naturally, the deeper plowing
of the soil provides more minerals from the depth of the earth for any plant cultivated, and gives better protection for the seed thus sawn. Hence, the agriculturist part of the story was not related whatsoever to the original myth about Tages.
Anatolian-Babylonian Haruspicy
From the previous data, it appears that the story about Tages originally had nothing to do with the discipline of haruspicy or divination by “liver-inspection”. Haruspicy was a non-Aryan non-Indo-European primitive and superstitious method of predicting the future events. There is nothing similar recorded in the Vedic texts. Man
has always been striving to predict the future events, frightened by the unknown and unpredictable. Even today, the official science of meteorology provide the weather forecast for several days, or even months in advance. The ancients Anatolians believed that they found a trustworthy method for prognosticating future by liver
and-etrails-inspection of the sacrificial animals. And the Etruscan/Latin term haru “liver” was undoubtedly a loan word from Babylonian. The discipline was widely popular throughout Near East in the 2
nd
and 1
st
millennium BCE, and is recorded even by the Hebrew Bible, the Book of Ezekiel 21:21:
“For the king of Babylon will stop at the fork in the road, at the junction of the two roads,
to seek an omen: He will cast lots with arrows, he will consult his idols, he will ָּראָּ ה בַּ כָּ בֵ ד
rā’āh bakkāḇ ȇ ḏ (bakkāβȇ ð) viz. inspect the liver.”
Otherwise, any form of divination has been prohibited by the Law of Moses, cf. Deuteronmy 18:10-12:
“There shall not be found among you ... that useth divination, an observer of times, or an enchanter, or a witch, or a charmer, or a consulter with familiar spirits, or a wizard, or a necromancer. For all that do these things are an abomination unto Yahweh.”
Etruscan stubborn adherence to decadent superstitious practice of haruspicy inherited

This actually confirms that the myth of Tages was an authentic tradition, and not a copy of the biblical account of the first man from the book of Genesis, though they share identical elements and overall concept which was markedly Aryan Indo
