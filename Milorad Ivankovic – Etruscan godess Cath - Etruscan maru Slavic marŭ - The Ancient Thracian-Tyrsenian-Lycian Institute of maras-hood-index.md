Etruscan maru Slavic marŭ:
The Ancient Thracian-Tyrsenian-Lycian Institute of maras-hood
Abstract
From an Italian or a German perspective, the extinct Etruscan tongue and related
Lemnian, Thracian and Lycian, are just that, the extinct dead languages, hence
remote, strange, mysterious. For the South Slavs, however, Etruscan with its
undeniable Slavic elements, is alive in its exclusive Slavic aspects, and still continues
to be the language of everyday communication. Thus, without recognition of the
Slavic elements in Etruscan, it is hardly possible to comprehend properly the Etruscan
language itself.

The Ancient Institute of maru
There was an important institute or office in Etruscan society represented by an
official called maru. The same sort of institution was also known to other ancient
people related to Etruscans, viz. Lycians, Thracians and Lemnians. However, the
related terms are not semantically congruent since they appear in different contexts
and with different shades of meanings.
LYCIAN: There is allegedly attested a related term marazi in Lycian tongue (spoken
in Asia Minor) in the sense of “judge“ with a different sufix (the letter z stands for the
affricate /ts/ as in modern German) with no confirmatory evidence in other languages.
Therefore, it cannot add anything useful to elucidate the Etruscan function of maru.
Thus, it is necessary to investigate further Thracian, Lemnian and Etruscan records
on the institute of maru.
THRACIAN: In the Odyssey, book 9th verse 197, there is mentioned an officiant
bearing a proper name apparently of the same origin as Etruscan maru. However, the
function of the officiant is designated by the Greek term ἱρεύς which is Ionian and
Epic form for the more common ἱερεύς meaning “priest, sacrificer, diviner,
minister“. Here's Odysseus' encounter with the priest:

It is clear from the above account that the priest called Maron was in the ministry of
(serving) the god Apollo who was in turn believed to watch over the Thracian village
of Ismarus. It is weird, however, to find that a Thracian priest bore the Etruscan
generic term for a specific social function as his personal proper name.
For the Greek form Μάρων, -ωνος, corresponds phonetically and morphologically to
maru and marunu recorded by Etruscan sources., e.g.:
arce . . . zilc marunuχva ten-θas
he made . . . presidency of marunuχva having held
“He (died), having held the presidency of the institute of maru”
Etruscan was somewhat primitive or rather 'simple' language, for it had no
grammatical gender, but only distinction between Human or Animate vs Non-Human
or In-Animate forms.



However, some Etruscan texts used the In-animate plural to designate animal terms.
Thus, the plural of Non-human Animate beings and In-animate objects likewise was
marked by the formant -χva if the steam of the noun ended in consonant, or -va if the
steam of the noun ended in vowel, the latter one being used in the example cited
above, viz. marunuχ (“ministry or office of a maru, viz. a priest, sacrificer, diviner,
cult official”) + va = plural “(several) offices, or ministries”. From the above example,
it becomes perfectly clear that there was more than one ministry, actually many of
then, in Etruscan society. This is supported by fact that the Thracian Maron was
specified exclusively as “ἱρεὺς the priest Ἀπόλλωνος of Apollo” by Homer in the
Odyssey. The Etruscans worshipped many divine entities, consequently there must
have been many cult ministries in ancient Etruria.
The maru of the Goddess Cath
Among those multiple Etruscan divities, there was a goddess invoked as ati Caθ, viz.
“Mother Cath” mentioned alongside with maru Caθsc “the minister maru of Cath”.
Thus analogously as the Thracian Μάρων functioned specifically as ἱρεὺς the
priest Ἀπόλλωνος of Apollo, there must have been a maru Caθsc “officiant of the
Cath cult” exclusively. The goddess Cath was considered the ruler of the 6th Region
of Heaven and as such is marked on the famous Etruscan bronze artifact known as the
Piacenza Liver, whence her epithet Celeritas solis filia "Swift (or Speedy) daughter
of the sun”. She was worshipped at Pyrgi harbor alongside with Ś uri or Suri, the same
divinity as Vedic Sū rya the “Sun”. The daughter of the Sun, actually ‘Dawn’ is most
naturally related to the Sun, lasting around 20 minutes at least and more (depending
on the observer’s latitude) at the Vernal Equinox, then swiftly “running away” after
being “chased” by the burning rays of the “pursuing” Sun.



Though her name is a cognate of Iranian Mithraic torch-bearer Cautes (related to
Greek καυστός or καυτός “burnt, red-hot”, cf. also Tocharian kaum- “Sun”)
representing the Sun of the Vernal Equinox, viz. the beginning of the New Year, in
Vedic texts appears impersonated rather by the goddess Uṣ as “Dawn”, also styled
Sū ryā a feminine form of Sū rya the Sun, meaning “Sunny, the Sun-maiden”, hence
called Sū ryasya duhitā “daughter of the Sun”. Thus, Etruscan Caθ (variants Caθa,
Cauθa, the latter matches phonetically the Iranian-Greek Cauta spelled Etruscan way,
syncopated and downsized into monosyllable Caθ), actually was Sū ryā the female
counterpart of the male Vedic god Sū rya [=Sū ria] worshipped at Pyrgi under the
Etruscanized form Suri as specified above.
Besides, there was mentioned the term maru in ministering other cults, for example
maru Paχa-θur-as “priest or minister of the devotees of Paχa or Βάκχος”. The
plural number denoting Human individuals in Etruscan was marked by the suffix -ar
if the steam of the noun ended in consonant, or -r if the steam of the noun ended in
vowel, e.g. ati > atii-ar “mothers”, clan > clen-ar “sons”, apa > apa-r “fathers”, but
when designating collectives or family names it was replaced by the suffux -θur
“belonging to (such and such family), being devotees, worshippers or followers of
(the god such and such) etc.”.
The Lemnian Office of maras
The ancient institute of maru was also mentioned on the Stele of Kaminia found 1884
near the village of the same name on the Greek Island of Lemnos. The funerary text
on the Stele was inscribed in honor of a man named Holaie from Phokia who held an
official position specified by the Lemnian term maras, resembling Etruscan maru,
and Lycian marazi.



The Stele of Kaminia Inscription, Lemnos, 6th century BCE

As is clearly demonstrated by the example of Thracian Maron, ἱρεὺς the priest
Ἀπόλλωνος of Apollo, the ministry of a maru or maras was a woved service of
lifetime. Apparently, Homer erroneously confused his lifetime function of a maru
with his proper name. It seems that the Lemnian term avi (Etruscan avil) too with no
specified number of years, analogously as Greek αἰών, might have rather denoted
“lifetime, all one's life long“.The maras-hood of Holaios was somehow related to his
spear (depicted on the stele and emphasized repeatedly two times in the inscription),
then his duty was likely to serve as a guard (kind of security staff or police officer) or
the like. The term maras being frozen in time, with no adequate specifications, can
hardly be understood in its proper meaning, how much less decoded its true origin.
The South Slavic marŭ
Fortunately, there is preserved an analogous lexeme, undoubtedly a cognate of the
related Etruscan, Lemnian and Thracian terms, still in everyday usage in Common
South Slavic which may elucidate their true origin and their authentic meanings.



The common root of all those technical terms denoting the function of maru, variants
maron, maras, marazi, is apparently mar- but from Etruscan, Thracian, Lemnian and
Lycian examples, it cannot be inferred what was actually meant by the signifier. There
is no preserved verbs, adjectives, participles in the records of those extinct languages.
However, the root √mar is an extremely productive lexeme in contemporary Common
South Slavic, inclusively of Slovenian.
The root-word mar itself in Common South Slavic denotes “CARE” < Old Slavic
marъ (an exact match of the Etruscan maru, since the Old Slavic ъ had the phonetic
value of the Indo-European short vowel u). That the term mar is not a loanword in
Slavic is proven by the existence of the opposite negative term ne-mar “NO-CARE,
care-less-ness” (in accord with the Aryan-Slavic principles of Vedic dialectics, viz.
every positive aspect must have its complementary opposite negative aspect), and the
multitude of its derivatives, e.g. mȃ ran “caring, attentive” vs nȅ -mā ran “care-less,
un-attentive”, with double prefix za-nè -mā ren “neglected, disregarded, unattended”,
má r-iti “to take care”, ne-má r-iti “not to take care”, za-ne-má r-iti “to neglect,
disregard, unattend”, za-ne-mar-í va-ti (iterative or frequentative of the previous
verb), verbal nouns má r-ē nje “caring, attentiveness” vs ne-má r-ē nje “un
attentiveness”, za-ne-mar-í vā -nje “neglect-ing, disregard-ing, unattend-ing”.
In Slovenian there is attested even specific development within the negative aspect of
the term, viz. ne marati (sb/sth) “dis-like”, ne maram to/tega/te “(I) don’t like
it/that/thee”, tega ne maram “(I) don't like that stuff“, besides its normal positive use,
edino staršema je mar “(literally) only of the elders (viz. parents, mom and dad) is
CARE“ viz. “the only people who care are Mom and Dad“. But unlike Common South
Slavic, Slovenian has preserved merely the noun mar “CARE“ of its positive aspect.



Conclusion
The Etruscan term maru, and its Lycian, Thracian and Lemnian variants marazi,
maron, maras, designating an ancient institute of maras-hood, in its essence
represented the fundamental idea preserved within the contemporary live and still in
use Slavic lexeme mar < Old Slavic marŭ .
The Slavic term mar by its basic meaning “CARE” seems to be the most appropriate
designator for ministry of any kind. Even today the term designates the same objective
entitled to modern institutions like “Health CARE ministry”. Thus, accordingly any
ministry whether past or contemporary, was/is a kind of social CARE with its officials
appointed to performing such a duty. For the fundamental requirement of an officiant
appointed to any ministry is to CARE for a specific domain of social activity, either
as an officiant in the service to god, or as a judge like Lycian marazi, or as a guard of
a prominent social figure, viz. a leader, head, chief, president, and the like.
