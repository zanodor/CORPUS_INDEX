Abstract
The religious worldview of the ancient Etruscans reveals their strong ties with India and Vedic culture. Moreover, the latest genetic studies have confirmed an uncontestable relationship between Indo-Aryans of the Pontic Steppe ancestry and the South European population of Toscana (Tuscany) in Italy, viz. the descendants of the original Etruscans. Notwithstanding the notorious superstitious and non-scientific Etruscan practice of haruspicy known by the Romans as the disciplina Etrusca, which was apparently of non-Aryan and non-Vedic origin, other important aspects of Etruscan culture and tradition seem to point unmistakably to the Aryan roots of the Etruscan people and language.
Keywords: Etruscan, cremation urns, swans, swastikas, numerals, haruspicy
1.Introduction
The Etruscan language has been attested on c.10,000 inscriptions from 7th century BCE to 1st century CE. The Etruscans engaged in Mediterranean trade after coming in contact with Euboean Greeks adopted Greek alphabet and adapted it to suit the phonological structure of their own tongue. The Etruscans used to write right to left, hence many of the Greek letters appears reversed in orientation. There is no surviving literature in Etruscan but mostly inscriptions associated with pictorial scenes, especially on sarcophagi and cremation urns, which prove that the Etruscans, just like Vedic Aryans, conceived of the afterlife as an extension of actual life.
Though there is a consensus reached among linguists and Etruscologists that Etruscan was not an Indo-European tongue but rather a Pre–Indo-European, and a Paleo-European language closely related to the Raetic language spoken in the Alps, and to the Lemnian language (the latter is found attested only in a few inscriptions on the Greek island Lemnos), the decipherment of the Etruscan is far from being successfully solved.
The main problem with the Etruscan inscriptions is the correct decipherment of the authentic phonetic value of the Etruscan letters. At the present stage of development, the mainstream Etruscologists have admitted that there is still much they do not know about the Etruscan language. It seems that the adherence to various scholarly prejudices and fixed ideas coupled with their own vanity is the main impediment on the way to the more correct and realistic interpretation of the Etruscan inscriptions.

2.Approved Genetic Ties of the Etruscans with Mother India
One of the latest investigations of the Y-chromosomal sequences of diverse Indian populations confirms that “A closest neighbor analysis in the phylogeny showed that Indian populations have an affinity towards Southern European populations... Surprisingly, the two South European populations (Toscani in Italia, TSI, and Iberian Population in Spain, IBS) are the closest neighbors of North Indian populations outside India” (Mondali et al. 2017). Thus, accordingly, it is hardly acceptable a theory about Etruscans and their language as being unrelated to Sanskrit and other Indo-European languages.
3.Ancient Cultural Ties between Etruscans and Vedic Aryans
The Aryan Vedic worldview has conceived the haṃsa < IE *ǵ hans ‘swan, goose’ (cf. Mayrhofer 1996:799) as symbolic for the human soul or spirit released after death from a deceased person, typified by the speckless white color of a swan or goose, and likewise assumed to be migratory like a goose. What is more, the haṃ sa is closely associated with and even considered the vehicle of the god of the dead, as is recorded in the scriptures. Although the Vedic god of the dead seems to be rather entitled in post-Vedic times as the Lord of the waters, anyway he is addressed with such an awe and dread expressive only in the presence of the former god of death. That Vedic
god was Varun ̣̣̣̣a, though later superseded by Yama (cf. Kuiper 1979:156,157), it was he and not Yama, who is propitiated by the following formula associated with the swan:
sarva-ambhasā ṃ patir devo Varuṇo haṃsa-vā hanaḥ pū jitaḥ prī timā n astu sa-samudra-nadī -nadaḥ
viz. “Lord of all the waters, god Varuna, who has a SWAN as his vehicle, may he who is thus worshipped be propitious to us, with ocean, and with all the (male & female) rivers and streams along.” (N ̄atỵ̣ aś ̄astra III 62-64).
Thus, there can be no doubt that in antiquity the Vedic Aryans considered Swan as symbolic of the soul or spirit of the deceased persons.
Analogously, the Swan as symbolic of the soul of a deceased Etruscan appears on the alabaster
urn with reclining male figure and kline-shaped chest with inscription, Chiusi, first half of the 3rd century BCE, inv. 13897 - Museo Gregoriano Etrusco - Vatican Museums (see FIGURE 1. below).

4.The ‘Swan Song’ and Transmigration of the Soul
Below the reclining figure there is a relief with four carved figures, two oriented left vs. two oriented right, the latter two being the mirror images of the former ones, actually depiction of a swan coupled with a winged male figure, undoubtedly representing domestic guardian spirit called Lar. The image clearly indicates the belief that Lar guards the soul of the deceased represented in the form of a swan.
The relief of the Etruscan alabaster urn displays the double mirror image of the Swan, likely to indicate the famous “Singing of the Swan” or better known the “Swan Song” as a metaphor for “Death”, referring to an ancient belief that swans sing a beautiful song just before their death, though being mostly silent during their lifetime.
Interestingly, this belief had become proverbial in ancient Greece by the 3rd century BCE about the same period of the date of the Etruscan alabaster urn. Aesop (1998:127) in his fable of The Swan and the Goose mentions the swan song legend as saving the very life of the bird, when it was caught by mistake instead of the goose, but was recognized by its song.
Even more instructive is Plato’s Phaedo (1966:84a-85b), for it quotes the words of Socrates, which actually confirm the common Aryan belief in transmigration of the soul:
“[84] And you seem to think I am inferior in prophetic power to the swans who sing at other times also, but when they feel that they are to die, [85a] sing most and best in their joy that they are to go to the god whose servants they are. But men, because of their own fear of death, misrepresent the swans and say that they sing for sorrow, in mourning for their own death. They do not consider that no bird sings when it is hungry or cold or has any other trouble; no, not even the nightingale or the swallow or the hoopoe which are said to sing in lamentation. I do not believe they sing for grief, nor do the swans; [85b] but since they are Apollo's birds, I believe they have prophetic vision, and because they have foreknowledge of the blessings in the other world they sing and rejoice on that day more than ever before.”
Thus the transmigration of the soul or metempsychosis as the quintessence of the common Aryan and likewise Etruscan worldview is perfectly attested by the Etruscan alabaster urn from Chiusi
(see FIGURE 1. below).

5.The Winged Lar
The Alabaster Urn from Chiusi is an artifact of special meaning and significance, illustrating perfectly the authentic Aryan concept of afterlife and the transmigration of the soul.
The initial phrase LARCE TUTNAŚ of the inscription might have been intended somehow
cryptically to indicate the presence of the same entity known to the Romans as Lar, plural Lares, “the household spirits watching over one’s family” (and also tutelary spirits watching over some public places). The term lar itself is related apparently also to Common South Slavic larmati “to make noise” (HJP 2021) and German lärmen “ibid.”, thus to denote originally the presence of a poltergeist “a kind of noisy ghost, the one making a noise by flapping his wings”. In any way, the terms seem to be semantically and phonetically related to each other.
The term TUTNAŚ itself apparently onomatopoeic in origin, most likely imitative of the sound of flapping wings, seems to be related to Sanskrit thuthu variant thū thū “the sound made in spitting” but also thutthu-kā raka “one who smacks his lips in eating”, thuthu-k̥rt “thuthu-maker, viz. Name of a bird” (Monier-Williams 1899:464). It resembles also Common South Slavic tutanj, tutnjati “make noise” < Old Slavic тѫтьнати, тѫтьнѣти tǫtьnati, tǫtьně ti “noise, thunder” > Old Russian тутьнати tutьnati “to thunder”; Russian тутень tutenь “noise, roar, the clatter of horses’ hoofs”; In this context, it seems to fit well the Sanskrit meaning in connection with the Slavic old custom of spitting in order to avert the evil spells. Accordingly, the noise
(made by flapping wings) of LAR seems to have had the same purpose as the Slavic spitting, viz. to protect the soul of the deceased against presumably evil spells, as is clearly indicated by the image on the relief carved above the inscription on the urn.
The two medial words of the inscription, viz. L ATh A L I Ś A, seem to be the most enigmatic. However, both might have been utilized as some forms of mantra or spell: the former is likely to be related to Sanskrit lā t ̣a “idle or childish language, repetition of words in the same sense but in
a different application” (Monier-Williams 1899:900); the latter seems to have conveyed originally the meaning of Sanskrit liś a in the compound ku-liś a “an axe, hatchet” but also “thunderbolt” (Monier-Williams 1899:296), thus fitting perfectly with “making noise” explained above. But it might have also meant to denote the same as Sanskrit derivative of liś a, viz. leś a “a small part or portion; a kind of song; a figure of speech in which a statement is made indirectly” (Monier-Williams 1899:903), appliable distinctly to the singing of the swan, viz. the child-like repetition of a uniform sound of his voice.
The last sequence allows a variant reading SGLABDA with medial voicing of consonants, hence it might have indicated verbatim what is depicted on the relief, viz. “Singing of the Swan”. For LABDA closely matches the Slavic labud “Swan”, preceded by SG a kind of prefix syncopated from Sa “accompanied by”, whereas Ga clearly imitates the natural sound of the Swan’s voice.

In the case of Etruscan alabaster urn from Chiusi, judging from the context, supported firmly by the relief carved above the inscription featuring the double mirror images of the Lar houshold spirit accompanied by the double mirror images of Swan, it becomes perfectly clear that there
must have been authentically much more at the stake, at least some form of protection spell and guidance to the soul of the deceased inscribed for the safe passage into the afterlife.
It is hardly imaginable that the ancient Etruscans did not develop some sort of protective spells or mantras, utilized during funerary rites and devoted for the safety of the souls of their beloved ones, on the journey into the next, viz. spiritual stage of existence, of which symbolic sign is undoubtedly the carved images of Swan.
6.Were the Etruscans also ‘Aryans’ ?
Most likely they thought about themselves as such. There are some words already established positively as Etruscan-Slavic cognates, among them is the very native Etruscan ethnic term by which they called themselves, viz. Ras, Rasna, Rasne, denoting Etruscans, Etruria. The same
ethnic Ras has been denoting the capital of the medieval Serb state of Raška, the etymology of which goes back to the root-word rasa “race, lineage, ancestral line, especially of noble breed“ whence also the adjectives mfn. rasni, rasna, rasno “purebred“ and the epithet rasni (esp. horse) “stud, a horse of particularly noble linegae fit for breeding“.
Professor Pešić (1995:40) related the ethnic Rase to Sanskrit rasa “the best or finest part or prime part of anything, essence, marrow; mercury, quicksilver (sometimes regarded as a kind of
quintessence of the human body, elswhere as the seminal fluid); semen virile“ (Monier- Williams 1899:869). Pešić also pointed out that the latter term left no trace in toponymy, hydromymy and anthroponymy, but the former is found widely disseminated across a vast territory of Southern Europe, especially in Slavic toponymy, such as Ras, Rasina, Rassina, Raška, Raška Draga, Resen, Resnica, Rasa, Rašo, etc. Thus, the Etruscans most likely considered also themselves the “purebred ones“, or the “Aryans“, but utilizing rather an Etruscan-Slavic cognate and widely disseminated and better known term Ras.

8.Basic Numerals as Additional Evidence
As already pointed out, there is attested a considerable number of cognate Indo-European words
in Etruscan, among them the basic numerals are of special interest.
The Etruscan basic numerals are undoubtedly Aryan Indo-European in origin. The famous
artifact known as the Tuscanian dice found 1848 in Toscanella, present-day Tuscania, Lazio
Region, Italy, comprises code-words for the first 6 numerals carved on its sides: θu 1, zal 2, ci 3,
huθ 4, maχ 5, śa 6 (see FIGURE 3. below).
The other numerals are found mostly inscribed on the tombs, sarcophagi and funerary urns,

9.The Etruscan Discipline
Of particular interest from the cultural point of view is the Etruscan practice of haruspicy, a form of divination literally “the inspection of the entrails” of sacrificed animals, especially the liver of the sacrificed sheep. Hence the practice of haruspicy from liver or reading the omens specifically from the liver is termed also as hepatoscopy or hepatomancy.

The practice of haruspicy relied prevalently on the sheep liver, which was considered actually a “map of heaven” divided into sections with inscribed names of Etruscan gods over particular section of liver/heaven, for decoding future events, in accordance with the Aryan Vedic principle “as above in heaven, so below on earth”.
The chief Vedic gods divine Agni and Soma have both celestial forms, viz. the Sun and the Moon having their abode in heaven, but also being present below in their terrestrial forms, the former as the Holy Fire, and the latter as the Sacred Drink among men on earth, in other words, “as above [viz. in heaven], so it is below [on earth]“. Analogously, the image of heaven is to be found projected onto the inner organs of animals, on liver in particular.
Despite utilizing the common Vedic principle “as above in heaven, so below on earth” the practice of haruscpicy is definitely non-Aryan and non-Vedic, but rather of Babylonian viz. Asia Minor origin, which actually supports the Herodotus’ theory of Anatolian Lydia as the once time homeland of the ancient Etruscan forefathers and ancestors, during their wanderings from the
Pontic Steppe and Central Asia, through Armenia and Anatolia, up to their final abode on
the Apennine peninsula.
