Chinese-and-Greek *Fo = Sanskrit bhā
As specified previously elsewhere, Chinese and Greek languages share some important common evolutionary points which make them both the members of the HU-family of languages. Besides, there are other additional common points found between Chinese and Greek significant to notice as the subject for further study.

There is Sanskrit prefix su- meaning “well, good” found often in compounds, e.g. su-bhaga m. su-bhagā f. “good dole, or having a good dole, viz. he of good fortune, or she of good fortune”.
However, in Sanskrit it appears in various degree of grading of the root vowel:
I) Zero or Empty Grade su- as above; II) Full Grade I or Guṇ a viz. Strengthened or Fulfilled Grade so- (= *sau-), e.g. so-ma < *su-mā “good change or measure, the one changing well, i.e. having good change or measure” denoting the Moon; cf. related Iranian form haoma.
III) Full Grade II or V ̥rddhi viz. Extended or Lengthened Grade sau- (=*sā ue.g. (viś va)-sau-bhaga “having a good dole or fortune for all”.
Thus, the prefix realized with sibilant S makes Sanskrit with Slavic and Latin the members of the SU-family of languages.
In Greek Chinese and Iranian however, it is realized with glottal spirant H which makes them the members of the HU-family of languages.
In Greek it appears in the following grades as follows:
I) Zero Grade: Greek hu-mno-s “hymn, viz. well thought” related to Sanskrit su-mna “ibid.” cf. Iranian hu-mata “good thought”. II) Full Grade II: Greek eu- < *heu- e.g. eu-logia “eulogy, viz. good or well made speech”.
In Chinese it is preserved also in its Full Grade II: hao “good”.
In Buddhism there is a cult of the future Buddha called Amitabhā < a-mita-bhā “infinite-light”. The same term is spelled in Chinese O-mi-to-Fo (Wade Romanization) vs E-mi-tuo-Fo (Pinyin Official Chinese Romanization), the last syllable Fo is Chinese spelling of thre Sanskrit syllable bhā meaning “light” but is used to denote the Buddha himself.
The Chinese syllable Fo matches exactly the Greek Phō (s) “light” (in Late New Greek pronounced Fo as in Chinese), found in various internationally adopted
Greek words, e.g. photo(s), photograph, photon(s), pronounced foto(s), fotograf, foton(s).
