Apparently, the ancient Celts too were acustomed to the usage of Etruscan letters as
is clearly evident from an Celtic artifact from the Etruscan era.
As is the well attested fact and known to the Etruscologists, the Celts of ancient
Europe used to share the same script with the Etruscans.
“Etruscan texts are among the earliest written objects from Italy, and the Etruscan
adaptation of the Greek alphabet was itself the source of a number of different local
alphabets. In the north, Etruscan was the basis for alphabets used for three well
attested languages: Lepontic, a Celtic language (distinct from Gaulish) spoken around
Lugano and attested in around 150 inscriptions from over thirty findspots in the Ticino
from the sixth century onwards; Rhaetic, a language believed to be related to
Etruscan, in which over one hundred, mostly very short, inscriptions are attested; and
Venetic, an Indo-European language attested in over two hundred inscriptions from a
range of sitesin theVeneto.” (Clackson 2014:709).
Such is an artifact from the Etruscan era, inscribed in the same manner as Etruscan
ones, but judging from the inscription itself, not of Etruscan but rather of Celtic origin.
Notwithstanding the letters KENOΦUNTOVIO incised on its surface vertically from
the top to the bottom, with an Venetic-like inverted “𐌡” for “U”, other specifications
point to the Celtic origin of the figure which apparently depicts an ancient druid Fire
priest featuring the characteristic Celtic shamrock design of his boots, and posed in the
common Aryan swastika pattern of his body extremities.

The swastika is symbolic not only of the primary celestial Light-entity in the
Universe, but generally represents its basic element or God AGNI the Fire, being
essentially the same as the Sun, viz. its representation on Earth. Agni is styled v ̥rṣ an
the “Bull” (Rig-Veda VII 3.3, etc.) of which the emblem is also the two horns on the
head of the figure of the Fire-priest above.
According to Aryan Vedic worldview, Agni the Fire is the Brahman or Priest of
the Gods, for he is the representative of the Gods among men on Earth. In other words,
the communication between humans and the gods is possible only through the
mediation of Agni the Fire, for he exclusively is capable of transmitting the sacrifice
directly to the realm of the gods.
Significantly, जिह्व jihva “the tongue” of Agni the Fire is invariably spoken of in
various Vedic texts, viz. His tongue is often mentioned (Rig-Veda VIII 72.l8 &c.). He
is also said to have three tongues (Rig-Veda III 20.2 ) or seven (Vā jasaneyi Saṃ hitā
17.79), his steeds also being seven-tongued (Rig-Veda III 6.2 ). A name was later given
to each of these seven tongues” (cf. Macdonell 1897:89).
