Krai Ukrai the Ukraine
The current wide popularity of the “Ukraine” theme gives the impression that it denotes ethnicity or nationality. However, the word itself is derived from an old common pan-Slavic adverb and authentically has a geographic designation only, hence it has nothing to do with either ethnicity or nationality.

The common Slavic term krai denotes “end, edge, border” (in accord with South Slavic orthography written kraj). In the South Slavic long tradition of folk poems it appears often with added prefix u-krai “aside, beside” (in modern orthography written ukraj), as in the phrases ukraj puta “at the edge of the path, aside or off the road”, ukraj vira “at the edge of wellspring, or beside the wellspring”, the latter especially being subject of
ancient poetic riddles.
There is a wonderful South Slavic folk poem (recorded in 19th century by S.Novaković) featuring the above mentioned motif:
Sedi vila ukraj vira čeka sina Tatomira, kad se rodi
da ga vodi.
“The fairy seated at the edge of wellspring, awaits her son Great-in-hiding, when he is born to guide him further on.”
The riddle solver is: “The Dawning/Evening Star (viz. Venus) and her son the Moon”.
Venus the brightest of the planets was known to the ancients as a wandering star, due to its shifting back and forth from the early hours of the eastern morning sky to the western sky in the early evening.
The Slavic compound name Tato-mir < Old Slavic Tatь “Thief” (a cognate with Sanskrit tāyu, variant stāyu “a thief, robber” < IE *√teh2-y- > Old Slavic verb tajiti “to hide”) + *měrъ “great, famous” (a cognate of German -mar in Volk-mar “famous among
the people”) denotes metaphorically the Moon (male in Sanskrit and Slavic) “great or skilled in hiding, viz. famous or great as a thief”, who goes hiding himself like a thief by the cover of the night.
Reference
